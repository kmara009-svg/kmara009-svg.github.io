# REFUEL. REBUILD. RETURN.

A scroll-driven presentation website for a RED-S rehabilitation pitch by **Kyle Marambio** (Sports Trainer & S&C Coach): a criteria-based recovery plan for Shantha, a 19-year-old national-team distance runner.

The content (every section, figure, table, number and APA reference) is taken verbatim from the `RED-S_Rehabilitation_Strategy.pptx` deck and lives in one file: `lib/content.ts`.

## Stack

Next.js (static export) · Tailwind CSS v4 · React Three Fiber + drei · GSAP ScrollTrigger · Framer Motion · Lenis.

## Run it

```bash
npm install
npm run dev      # http://localhost:3000
npm run build    # static site in ./out
```

Pushing to `main` deploys to GitHub Pages through `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` (set the repository's Pages source to "GitHub Actions").

## Presenting (screen recording)

* Every stop is a 1920×1080 stage, scaled to fit the window. Record at 1920×1080 for a 1:1 layout.
* `→` `↓` `space` `enter` advance, `←` `↑` go back, `Home` / `End` jump. The 3D story has six beats; each slide is one stop.
* The story opens on a bird's-eye view of the whole oval with the titles, then descends onto her running before the X-ray.
* Every slide lives on a billboard beside the track at its marker. When you move on, the slide shrinks back onto its billboard and dissolves into it while the camera backs straight off the sign, swings round behind her as she sets off, and follows her run (pulling back a little mid-run). As she halts in her lane just before the next sign (run, walk and idle clips blend by her speed), the camera swings onto that sign and closes in until its face exactly fills the screen, and the next slide grows out of it with its entrance animations. Going back plays the same move in reverse. She crosses the finish line for the closing slide (the camera orbits round her as she does), then jogs on to a last sign for the references, where her coach is waiting. He hangs a gold medal round her neck, the two of them turn to the camera, and the camera closes in on the sign for the references, shown all at once. Keyboard trips take about six and a half seconds and start moving on the first frame; the last, with the medal, takes twelve. Pressing again mid-trip carries straight on.
* Press `P` for presentation mode: fullscreen, cursor hidden, navigation chrome hidden. `Esc` leaves it.
* The top-right 600×400 px of every stop holds the presenter's recorded speech for that stop. It comes up and plays once the page has come to rest on the stop, and fades the moment you move on (so it is hidden during every run) or when it finishes. Speeches 1 to 4 cover the six opening stops (3 and 4 each span two stops and carry on where they paused); Speeches 5 to 24 are slides 1 to 20; the references have none. The videos live in `public/speeches` (`lib/speeches.ts` maps stops to files): the recordings re-encoded to 720p for the web, which looks the same in the box at a quarter of the data, with their sound copied unchanged, except Speech 1's, raised about 15 dB to match the rest. The untouched 1080p originals are in the repository's `speeches` release. Open `/?qa=1` to see the zone outlined.
* The site records itself: press `S` (or click "record" in the bottom-right), choose this tab and Share, and it records the screen and the speeches' sound until you press `S` again (or the browser's "Stop sharing"), then saves `RED-S presentation <date time>.mp4`, 1920×1080, to the downloads folder. The timer beside the button says when a take passes 15 minutes. The sound is taken straight from the speech videos, so nothing else playing on the computer gets in. MP4 needs Chrome or Edge; other browsers save a WebM and say so (`components/Recorder.tsx`).
* To record: reload the page, press `S` and share this tab (or start another screen recorder), press `P`, then press `R`. The speech of the stop the page opens on waits for `R`, and every later one starts by itself; `R` also restarts the current stop's speech from the top. Only the speech you hear and the next one ever load, and the 3D scene stops drawing while a slide covers it, so the video gets the laptop and the connection.
* All text is 28 px or larger at 1920×1080.

## The 3D story

* **Stadium**: Lakeside Stadium, South Melbourne, at dusk with the floodlights on. A full 400 m oval (two 84.39 m straights, 37.1 m bends, eight lanes) in Lakeside's blue, round a striped football pitch with its markings, goals and a hammer cage on the blue D's. The main stand on the home straight sits under a big roof with deep steel trusses on top and the sports centre behind it; the long, low white far stand has a thin canopy with a light strip under its edge; the red-brick 1926 grandstand on the west bend has its timber benches full too. Between them they hold about 6,900 instanced spectators (per-seat colours, a vertex-shader bob). Four floodlight masts with lamp frames and a big screen stand round the ground. The stands, roofs, masts and buildings are merged, vertex-coloured geometry, so the whole venue draws in a few calls.
* **Albert Park**: the lake wraps the stadium to the north-east, with two wooded islands, about 50 dinghies and a water shader that mirrors the dusk sky at grazing angles. Around it are the ring road and the shore path with their lamps, about 700 parkland trees, palm-lined drives, the aquatic centre (MSAC), a car park, and the streets of South Melbourne beyond. The park dims away from the floodlights. North, across the lake, is the Melbourne skyline: one instanced draw for every tower (Eureka's gold crown, Australia 108, the Arts Centre spire), with windows from one mipmapped texture and hazed into the sky behind. A gradient sky dome with afterglow and clouds, fog in the same palette, a high key light standing in for the floodlights (its shadow camera follows the runner), and image-based lighting. Everything is procedural; there are no external textures. The new venue draws in fewer calls and fewer triangles than the old one.
* **Runner**: a textured, rigged Mixamo character driven by a motion-captured Mixamo run clip. The model's own legs were wide trouser tubes; they are replaced by bare legs lofted from anatomical cross-sections (calf heads, shin, patella, knee hollow, vastus medialis, Achilles, slim ankles), tucked into the shorts hem with the shorts' own skin weights and into the shoes, and skinned with a knee blend that is long in front and short behind (`scripts/model/rebuild-legs.mjs`). Her face and hair are rebuilt too (`scripts/model/rebuild-face.mjs`): the slit eyelids are filled with a smooth patch and the face gets a texture of its own, painted with big glossy brown eyes, lashes, full brows, blush and peach lips; the old hair puffs are replaced by sleek pulled-back hair with a centre parting and a bun, an orange and brown wax-print headband with a bow, and ears with gold studs. The clip was retargeted onto her skeleton offline (per-bone rest-pose offsets, then `SkeletonUtils.retargetClip`) and stored as `public/models/run.json`. To use a different character, export a Mixamo rig as GLB, retarget the clip onto it the same way and point `components/three/Runner.tsx` at the new files.
* **The race**: four rivals run the lap with her, one in each of lanes 2 to 5. They are the same rigged model in a lighter copy (`rival.glb`, about 15,000 triangles against her 50,000) and run on the same mocap clips, blended by their own speed. Each has her own skin tone, hair colour, kit and headband, recoloured in the shader from her textures. A mask (`rival-kit.png`) marks skin, top, shorts and shoe accents; it is baked offline from the body texture's colours and from where on her each texel is worn. Their places are a pure function of the scroll, like hers (`lib/race.ts`): a gap to her at each slide, eased across each run. Early on the field is ahead of her; through the recovery slides she reels them in; she leads into the final straight and crosses the line first, and the others finish behind her in real time while the closing slide comes up. The gaps keep clear of the chase camera, which rides behind her right shoulder. A rival that would come within 1.2 m of the camera is hidden, and rivals out of view are skipped altogether. A planned change of place happens only while she is out of view, and stepping back off the finish line winds the run-in back with the scroll.
* **The coach and the medal**: her coach waits beside the references sign, holding her medal. He is built from her model (`scripts/model/coach.mjs`), so the same rig and idle clip drive him. The bun, headband and studs are gone and the hair is cut short with some height on top. The chest is flattened, the waist straightened and the hips narrowed, and the sleeves and trouser legs stand off the skin. He also gets a shirt collar, open at the throat, and his own face texture (`coach-face.mjs`): smiling eyes, heavy brows, an open smile and a short dark beard. In the app he wears her textures, recoloured in the shader by bind-pose position: a navy gingham shirt (box-filtered, so it greys out evenly with distance), dark trousers, a belt, brown shoes, his skin tone, and dark brown hair faded short at the sides. The last section is twice as long as the others. She halts in front of him, and over the extra scroll a two-bone IK on top of his idle clip lifts the ribbon over her head and lowers it onto her shoulders. The ribbon is a strip rebuilt each frame through six points, which slide from his hands to points on her rig as it settles. Then he lets go, the two of them turn to the camera, and it closes in on the sign. All of it follows the scroll (`lib/ceremony.ts`), so stepping back takes the medal off her again. Without WebGL there is no ceremony to show, so the last section is an ordinary trip's length again.
* **X-ray**: her body fades to a translucent glow while capsule bones parented to her real rig bones light up. The pelvis, sacrum, femoral neck, tibia and foot bones use the lime "high-risk" material.
* **Bone**: trabecular bone is a warped gyroid isosurface polygonised with three's `MarchingCubes`. Scroll progress drives the trabecular thickness and a grain term that perforates the network, so it thins to an osteoporotic lattice and rebuilds again.
